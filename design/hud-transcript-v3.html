HUD transcript — iteration 3: the reveal, metered

The three durations on the last board were three guesses at one number, and all three animated the burst — the words that arrive together when a snapshot lands — when the thing worth animating is the speech. This board releases words at the rate they were actually spoken, and derives the blur from that.

R = max(2, words in take ÷ seconds of audio)   the engine gives this free — it already knows both numbers
slot = 1 / R   conversational English: 150 wpm → 2.5 words/s → 400 ms
D = clamp(0.72 / R, 0.15, 0.40)   → at 2.5 words/s, D = 288 ms
0.72 because a word's entrance should occupy about two thirds of its slot: less and consecutive words read as simultaneous; more and two are always mid-blur, which is a haze rather than a reveal. The bounds are perceptual, not stylistic: under ~150 ms the eye reads a cut rather than a blur, and past 400 ms the word is still resolving when the next one lands.
D = 288 ms at 2.5 words/s — the derived default
Metered
One word per slot, in the rhythm of speech rather than the rhythm of polling. The queue absorbs a burst, so nothing ever lands in a lump and nothing has to be hidden by the animation.

Why meterWords arrive in bursts because transcription is polled, not because speech is lumpy. A queue released at R turns a 4-word burst into four arrivals 400 ms apart, which is what the ear heard anyway. The burst then has no upper bound to worry about: seven words after a slow snapshot simply take 2.8 s to drain.
Where R comes fromThe engine returns text and the take has a duration, so R is measured rather than assumed — a slow speaker gets slow words, a fast one keeps up. Clamped to 2–6 words/s so a mis-estimate cannot make the line crawl or blur past legibility.
InkThe newest word or two sit at 66 % until the next snapshot confirms them, then settle. Each snapshot re-transcribes the audio so far, so a word can still change. A word that looks final but moves reads as a mistake; one that looks provisional reads as thinking.
Snapshot interval~1.2 s to start, stretching as the engine cost grows (0.59 s at 8 s of audio, about 2.5 s at two minutes). The reveal rate is independent of it, which is the point of metering.
TestableThe reveal schedule is arithmetic on a queue — word i appears at i·slot, each with duration D — so it can be unit-tested like the rest of the HUD's lifecycle rather than judged by eye.
